# Historical Note: Why the BSRA Collection Matters

*An editorial overview of the BSRA archive and its place in the history of the UFO era.*

The conventional history of the UFO phenomenon begins on June 24, 1947, when Kenneth Arnold reported a very fast flying formation of nine shiny crescent-shaped objects skipping past Mount Rainier and an Oregon newspaperman coined the phrase "flying saucer." Everything that followed — the organizations, the theories, the contactees, the government correspondence — is usually told as a reaction to that event and the national wave it triggered in early July.

The documents in this collection tell a different story. They show that at least one organization did not react to the saucer era. _It was waiting for it._

## The machine was already running

By the time Arnold landed his plane, the Borderland Sciences Research Associates of San Diego had been in continuous operation for over two years. Founded by Meade Layne in 1945, BSRA had a subscriber network of research associates, two periodicals in regular production (*The Round Robin*, from February 1945, and *The Flying Roll*, from March 1946), a working contact methodology built around the trance mediumship of Mark Probert, and — most importantly — a complete, published cosmology explaining what strange aerial objects were and where they came from: "ether ships" from Etheria, an adjacent plane of existence, piloted by beings who could materialize and dematerialize at will.

None of this was improvised after the fact. The seance transcripts, newsletter articles, and doctrinal statements collected here were mimeographed and mailed to subscribers throughout 1945 and 1946, when "flying saucer" was not yet a phrase in the English language.

## October 1946: the dress rehearsal

On the evening of October 9, 1946, during the Giacobini-Zinner meteor shower, Mark Probert and other San Diegans [reported a strange winged object over the city](https://www.newspapers.com/article/hanford-morning-journal-kareeta/153063454/). What happened next is the clearest evidence of an organization with its apparatus already in place. Probert and others telephoned Layne while the object was still in view; Layne advised telepathic contact; and within days a fully formed narrative existed — a peaceful craft called the "Kareeta," piloted by advanced visitors who wished to meet with Earth's scientists, in an isolated area, but feared the reception they would receive. Probert also channeled that the Oct. 1946 appearance was an experiment and that the phenomena would return within a year. Subscribers were told to keep their eyes on the skies.

On October 14, 1946, the wire services carried the story nationally, and it ran in papers as far away as Australia. Every element of what would later be called the contactee movement — the benevolent visitors, the telepathic channel, the peace message, the wary approach to a hostile humanity — was in print in *The Round Robin* ("Welcome, Kareeta!", V2 N10, [in this collection](https://archive.org/details/the-round-robin-v-2-n-10-oct-1946/page/3/mode/2up)) more than eight months before Arnold, and six years before George Adamski's celebrated desert contact of 1952.

## July 1947: instant deployment

When the national disc wave crested in the first week of July 1947, BSRA needed to invent nothing. On July 8, 1947 — two weeks after Arnold, one day after, or during the [peak of the summer of 1947 wave](https://medium.com/@richgel99/the-radar-rockets-nukes-and-uaps-timeline-e3aa1dad013d), and on the same day as the Roswell crash announcements in west coast newspapers — Layne's ["Memorandum of Importance"](https://archive.org/details/upsampled_bsra_memorandum_6751_july_8_1947) (the document later famous as "Memorandum 6751" in the FBI's declassified files) was dispatched to authorities, confidently explaining the discs in language drawn directly from the group's existing Round Robin, Flying Roll, and seance literature. No organization starting from zero produces a doctrinally complete position paper for federal officials in fourteen days. BSRA could, because the paper had effectively been written for two years; only the addressees were new.

The pattern continued through the documents collected here: the [1950 letter to President Truman](https://archive.org/details/500705-letter-from-meade-layne-to-harry-s-truman), the [mailing list that included military leadership](https://medium.com/@richgel99/bsras-private-distribution-list-from-1950-a2a2e0c27aee), the [1951 letter to the Atomic Energy Commission](https://archive.org/details/bsra-a-letter-to-the-aec-may-19-1951), the [Aug. 1952 letter to authorities](https://archive.org/details/bsra_message_to_president_late_aug_1952/mode/2up) warning them to "not shoot!" days after the July 1952 DC wave, the [1957 open letter to Congress](https://archive.org/details/nicap_feb_6_1957_bsra_open_letter/page/n1/mode/2up). The playbook — sighting, psychic inquiry, publication, direct appeal to authority — had been run first in October 1946 and was simply repeated at scale.

## Why this reorders the standard history

Two claims that usually appear late in UFO historiography turn out, on the evidence of this archive, to have been there from the beginning:

**The interdimensional hypothesis predates the extraterrestrial hypothesis in organized form.** Layne's "4-D" Etherian framework — usually treated as a 1960s–70s development associated with Jacques Vallée and John Keel, arising from the failures of nuts-and-bolts ufology — was in fact the *first* organized interpretation of the phenomenon, published before the phenomenon had a name. The ETH (Extraterrestrial Hypothesis) displaced it, not the other way around.

**The contactee template predates the contactees.** The Kareeta episode of 1946 contains the complete genre in miniature, years before the figures normally credited with creating it.

## A longer lineage

None of this came out of nowhere — and neither did the phenomenon. Anomalous objects in the sky were not new in 1947, or in 1946. Charles Fort had spent decades compiling published reports of unexplained aerial objects, from _The Book of the Damned_ (1919) through _New Lands_ (1923) and _Lo!_ (1931), drawn from newspapers and scientific journals reaching back into the nineteenth century. When the discs made headlines, more than one observer pointed out that Fort had been reporting such things a generation earlier. The saucer era did not mark the arrival of a phenomenon; it marked the moment the mainstream press finally noticed one that Fortean researchers had been cataloguing all along.

BSRA stood squarely in that lineage. Layne was a working Fortean — the _Round Robin_ was a bulletin of "borderland facts and happenings," covering the full range of anomalies Fort had legitimized as objects of study — and he joined it to a second older tradition, the etheric physics of Sir Oliver Lodge and the broader spiritualist cosmology of the preceding decades. That tradition included _Oahspe_ (1882), the channeled bible of John Ballou Newbrough, whose etherean heavens and fleets of airborne vessels crewed by advanced beings supplied a vocabulary — and a precedent for channeled cosmology itself — that BSRA drew on explicitly in works like [_The Ether Ship Mystery and Its Solution_](https://archive.org/details/the-ether-ship-mystery-and-its-solution-flying-discs-meade-layne-1950). That is why BSRA could be operational before the 1947 wave: it was not a group that lucked into an old framework for a new phenomenon. It was a continuation of an existing research program, tracking a phenomenon that was already on the record, with an interpretive apparatus already built. The year 1947 changed the audience, not the subject.

Readers may judge the content of these publications as they see fit. What is not in dispute is the paper trail: dated, mimeographed, mailed, in some cases officially recorded and later FOIA'd, and now scanned and archived for posterity — showing that the investigation of these objects, and the machinery for interpreting them, was already running in San Diego before the world learned the words "flying saucer."

That is what this collection preserves, and why it was worth years of effort to assemble.
